White Elephant rules: how to play

A White Elephant gift exchange is a party game where everyone brings one wrapped gift, and on your turn you can either open a new gift or steal one that's already been opened. Here are the classic rules, the common variations, and the questions that always come up mid-game.

The rules in 30 seconds

  1. Everyone brings one wrapped gift within the agreed budget and puts it in a pile.
  2. Draw a random order. Number 1 opens a gift from the pile.
  3. On each following turn, the player either opens a new gift or steals any gift that's already open.
  4. If your gift is stolen, you immediately open a new gift or steal a different one. Your turn ends only when someone opens a new gift.
  5. A gift freezes after 3 steals — whoever holds it then keeps it.
  6. Final swap: when every gift is open, player 1 gets one chance to swap with anyone.

Before the party

Set a budget

Pick one number and share it with the invite. $20–$30 is the most common range for friends and coworkers; family groups often go lower. A clear budget matters more than its size: one $100 gift in a $20 game ruins the balance of every steal that follows.

Decide the rules ahead of time

Most arguments at a White Elephant are about stealing: how many times, and whether you can steal back. Agree before the party — put the rules in the invite — and nobody has to referee in the middle of the game.

Wrap it so nobody can guess

Disguising the shape is half the fun. A small gift in a big box, or a bottle wrapped as a cylinder, makes the opening gift a real gamble.

How turns work

Draw numbers from a hat, or let an app shuffle the order. Player 1 has no choice: there is nothing to steal yet, so they open a gift and show it to the room. From player 2 on, each person chooses:

  • Open a wrapped gift from the pile, or
  • Steal any gift that is already open.

The person who loses a gift goes right away — they open a new one or steal a different one. This chain can run for several steals, and it always ends the same way: someone opens a new gift from the pile. Then the next number in the order plays.

All three are optional. Small groups sometimes play with no steal limit; big office parties almost always freeze gifts, or the game never ends.

White Elephant, Dirty Santa, Yankee Swap: what's the difference?

Mostly the name. White Elephant is the most common term in the US. Dirty Santa is popular in the South and usually implies more stealing and gag gifts. Yankee Swap is the New England name, and its version of the final swap is the best known. You'll also hear Thieving Secret Santa, Grinch Gift Exchange and Chinese Gift Exchange — same game, same rules, with small house variations.

It is not the same as Secret Santa: in Secret Santa each person secretly buys for one assigned person, and there is no stealing.

Questions that come up mid-game

Can you steal a gift back right after it was taken from you?

Under the classic rules, no — you have to wait until someone opens a new gift. Some groups allow it; just decide before you start.

How many times can a gift be stolen?

Usually three. On the third steal the gift is frozen with its new owner.

Can you steal a gift that was just opened?

Yes. A freshly opened gift is fair game for the very next player.

What's the best position?

Last. The final player sees every gift before choosing. That's why the final swap exists for player 1.

How long does it take?

Plan about 2–3 minutes per player. A group of 15 takes 30–45 minutes, depending on how dramatic the steals get.

Does everyone have to play?

Only people who brought a gift. The number of gifts must equal the number of players, or someone ends up empty-handed.
